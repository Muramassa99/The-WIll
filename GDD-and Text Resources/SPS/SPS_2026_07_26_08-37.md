### Locked Anchor And Contact Contract

The red-dot anchor:

- is always inside the resolved profile;
- is the mouse/surface-hit position for Volume Stroke;
- is the point through which a surface-bound spline passes;
- determines how deeply the profile overlaps the existing drawing surface;
- must not be tangent to the final outline.

New mandatory anchor clearance:

```text
minimum distance from anchor to final resolved boundary = 0.0005 m
```

The valid anchor region is therefore the final profile inset inward by
`0.0005 m`.

Preserve existing deformation behavior:

- if shape deformation does not violate the inset region, leave the anchor
  unchanged;
- if deformation pushes the inset region through the anchor, move the anchor to
  the nearest valid inset position;
- interactive anchor dragging uses the same inset;
- perpendicular/contact calculations still use the real exterior boundary, not
  the inset boundary.

This guarantees at least `0.0005 m` overlap when the anchor is placed on the
drawing surface. The value is intentionally tunable after testing.

### Locked Save-Time Contact Resolution

The expensive profile-local contact decision occurs during profile saving, not
during drawing.

Save-time steps:

1. resolve the final outline including fillets;
2. validate/push the anchor into the `0.0005 m` inset;
3. find the shortest anchor-to-real-boundary path;
4. resolve equal-distance ambiguity using the clock rule;
5. save the compiled local contact data with the profile.

Clock rule for equal-distance candidates:

1. the bottom arc `3 -> 6 -> 9` outranks the top arc
   `9 -> 12 -> 3`;
2. the left arc `6 -> 9 -> 12` outranks the right arc
   `12 -> 3 -> 6`;
3. a perfectly centered symmetrical case resolves to 6 o'clock.

Equivalent continuous reading:

- prefer the tied direction closest to local 6 o'clock;
- for a perfect mirror tie, prefer the local-left candidate.

Compiled data should preserve at least:

- anchor position;
- local closest/contact point;
- normalized local contact direction;
- anchor-to-boundary distance;
- a rule/schema version for future migration.

Existing saved profiles that do not contain compiled contact data may be
upgraded once when loaded/resaved. They must not repeatedly solve the boundary
problem during a stroke.

### Locked Runtime Placement Contract

Runtime use should perform only the work needed for material and placement:

- use the selected material's existing `.tres`-driven data;
- use the existing hit position and surface normal;
- use the compiled saved-profile geometry/contact data;
- generate or reuse the required visuals/CSG geometry;
- align the local contact direction toward the surface-inward direction;
- maintain the profile's perpendicular frame;
- apply the user rotation bias after automatic perpendicular alignment;
- preserve local profile scale.

For a surface-bound spline:

- the profile plane remains perpendicular to the spline tangent;
- changing surface normals are allowed to roll the profile continuously around
  that tangent;
- the resulting material path may spiral/screw around the surface to preserve
  perpendicularity.

For Volume/CSG Material Stroke:

- successive placements follow the changing surface frame;
- the profile may rotate screw-like while the mouse moves;
- the anchor remains on the mouse hit while the saved contact direction points
  into the existing material.

The saved local closest wall is not reselected at every sample. The world
placement frame moves and rotates around the already compiled local profile.

### Performance Boundary

The new orientation itself should remain constant-time transform work.

Do not perform the following in the raw mouse-motion hot path:

- final-boundary scanning;
- fillet resolution;
- polygon rebuilding;
- triangulation;
- saved-resource loading;
- profile rescaling;
- repeated profile compilation.

Preferred runtime shape:

```text
select/recall saved profile
    -> load/compile cached profile data once
mouse/spline update
    -> existing hit data + lightweight orientation transform
accepted deposit
    -> material/visual/CSG placement
```

Use Godot's profiler/frame-time monitors plus focused counters when testing.
Frame-time spikes and drawing smoothness matter more than average FPS alone.
Do not add permanent per-frame logging that creates its own stutter.
